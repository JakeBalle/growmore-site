The science

Fertiliser decisions

Possibly — it depends on how far from optimal your soil test results currently are. If nutrient levels are already well managed, the immediate benefit is price comparison. If levels are below optimal, there's likely genuine production to be gained from a well-planned programme.

Between 12 and 20 t/ha of DM would be a good range for most NZ pastoral farms. Less when droughts persist or a wet spring with lack of sunlight hinders recovery after grazing. Top farmers feeding 70% or more home-grown pasture regularly achieve 1,100–1,700 kg MS/ha with 600 kg MS/cow.

In a nutshell, enough to replace what is leaving the farm. Or, if the nutrient levels are below optimal, enough to build them up — after covering what is leaving — or as much as your budget permits. Soil tests and labs

You can use your existing nutrient advisor, an independent agronomist, or do it yourself for a truly independent result. The Fertiliser Association of New Zealand has a helpful instruction manual at fertiliser.org.nz/resources/booklets.

Any lab able to run the suite of tests adopted as standards in New Zealand is fine. International labs are acceptable too, but some results may need to be translated into NZ equivalents which can introduce a further margin of error.

NZ soil science is built on empirical studies using the Olsen P lab extract. The calibration details — how many kilograms of phosphorus are required to change the test result, and what production change is linked to that — are all linked to the Olsen P extract. If you only have a Mehlich 3 result, it would need to be converted to an Olsen P equivalent, which introduces additional uncertainty.

The app can perform a weighted average on your soil test results. Use the hectare slider to apportion each soil test result accordingly. So if you have a 100 hectare block with 3 samples covering 20ha, 30ha and 50ha, the 20ha sample won't unduly skew the result. Alternatively, for a non-weighted result, set each sample to the full area of the block and the app will apply a uniform average.

The app can only deal with single soil types when calculating a fertiliser application. You can choose to make a single fertiliser application cover more than one block provided they are the same soil type. If they are different, you should consider creating a separate fertiliser application for each soil type.

Technical options

BCSR stands for Base Cation Saturation Ratio. It's an alternative interpretation of soil cation balance — calcium, magnesium, potassium and sodium — expressed as percentages of the total cation exchange capacity. It has been persistent in certain agronomy circles and is included as an option. It often results in higher levels of magnesium being recommended. User discretion is advised — it should be understood as an alternative lens, not a replacement for SLAN-based recommendations.
